T1 Getting there: South Lake Tahoe is about 190–200 miles from San Francisco, roughly 3.5–4 hours via I-80 then US-50 (Friday traffic adds an hour or more; chain controls much more) [check: sources give 188–201.5 mi]. US-50 is the direct road to the South Shore (narrower, mostly one lane each way over Echo Summit); I-80 goes to Truckee and the North Shore (about 187 miles from San Francisco), a wider road that usually stays open longer in storms. Check a live map before you leave.
T2 The lake: about 1,645 ft at its deepest, the second-deepest lake in the US after Oregon's Crater Lake; surface about 6,225 ft above sea level; about 72 miles of shoreline [check: sources say 71–75 mi, and it varies with lake level]. DID YOU KNOW: scientists have measured its clarity with a white Secchi disk since 1968: about 102 ft that first year, about 72 ft as the 2022 average (UC Davis).
T3 South Lake Tahoe and Stateline: the California–Nevada line crosses US-50 at Heavenly Village; on the Nevada side, Stateline's casino hotels are all within a short walk of the village. The Heavenly Gondola climbs from the village plaza; summer sightseeing ran about 10:00–17:00 daily in summer 2025 [check: summer hours]; ticket prices: see skiheavenly.com (no figure given; Epic Pass holders ride free).
T4 Emerald Bay State Park and Vikingsholm: Vikingsholm is reached by a STEEP 1-mile trail from the lot on Highway 89 (or a gentler 1.5-mile trail from Eagle Point Campground); parking about $10 per vehicle per day or $3 per hour, sunrise to sunset; the lot fills and can close in peak season (then park at Eagle Falls or D.L. Bliss). House tours ran daily May 23 – September 30, 2026, every half hour 10:30–16:00, first come, first served; adults about $18 (Sierra State Parks Foundation, 2026) [check: one older guide says $10]. No dogs on the Vikingsholm trails. DID YOU KNOW: Lora Knight bought the bay in 1928 and built the 38-room Scandinavian-style house in 1929 with about 200 workers, designed by Swedish architect Lennart Palme; she also had a small stone teahouse built on Fannette Island, Lake Tahoe's only island.
T5 Highway 89 around Emerald Bay (the winter box): this stretch closes after heavy snow and avalanches, sometimes for days (Caltrans closures in March 2025, and slides in earlier winters). In winter, check Caltrans QuickMap before planning Day 1 or Day 2 around it [check: seasonal, no fixed dates].
T6 D.L. Bliss State Park and the Rubicon Trail (just north of Emerald Bay): reopened for camping and day use on May 21, 2026, after a multi-year waterline project; day use sunrise to sunset, about $10 per vehicle; off-season day use (closed to vehicles) from October 1, 2026. The Rubicon Trail runs along the shore toward Emerald Bay [check: length given as 6.5 or 7.4 miles; dogs not allowed on the beach or trails per the park page].
T7 The West Shore and Sugar Pine Point: the Hellman-Ehrman Mansion (1903) in Ed Z'berg Sugar Pine Point State Park; house tours daily May 23 – September 30, 2026, on the half hour 10:30–15:30, about 45 minutes, first come, first served (Sierra State Parks Foundation, 2026) [check: tour price not in the sources found].
T8 Tahoe City: the Gatekeeper's Museum stands beside the Lake Tahoe Dam at the lake's only outlet, the Truckee River (130 West Lake Blvd); its first cabin housed the gatekeeper who set the water flow out of the lake. Open about April 1 – October 31, Wednesday–Sunday 11:00–16:00 (2026 listings), by appointment in winter; the Marian Steinbach Indian Basket Museum is part of it. Admission: see northtahoemuseums.org (no figure given).
T9 Sand Harbor (Nevada State Park, East Shore, an option from the North Shore): from April 15 to October 15, a day-use reservation is required to enter by car between 8:00 and 10:30 (a $5 processing fee in advance; same-day free until 10:29); after 10:30, first come, first served. Entry about $10 for Nevada-plated cars and $15 for others (2025) [check: 2026 fees not published in the sources found].
T10 Truckee: the town grew up around the Central Pacific Railroad, which arrived in 1868, when the stop was renamed Truckee; Commercial Row's brick buildings mostly date from the 1870s–1880s, and the Commercial Row–Brickelltown Historic District was listed on the National Register in 2009.
T11 Donner Memorial State Park (Truckee): day use sunrise to sunset, about $10 per vehicle; the visitor center is open daily 10:00–17:00, its museum and gift shop through the winter too (closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day; California State Parks); the park is open year-round, but its campgrounds closed for the 2026 season on Oct 13, 2026 (Sierra State Parks Foundation update 10/13/26), with the lakefront paved trail still open. DID YOU KNOW: the Donner Party, about 81–87 emigrants, was trapped here by early snow in late 1846; the first relief reached them in mid-February 1847, and about 45 survived [check: counts differ by source; use 'about'].
T12 Palisades Tahoe (Olympic Valley, between Tahoe City and Truckee): the resort hosted the VIII Winter Olympic Games in February 1960 [the television-firsts claim was cut: it had no source with an address]; it was renamed Palisades Tahoe on September 13, 2021, after consultation with local Washoe groups.
T13 Getting around without a car: two free, on-demand shuttles. TART Connect covers North Lake Tahoe and Truckee (runs in summer and winter seasons; no dates given, the book says to check its site); Lake Link covers South Lake Tahoe and Stateline (book in its app; hours: see its site, no figure given). They don't connect. [no source found for a bus joining the north and south shores; the book says to check the transit sites]
T14 Winter driving (the winter box): Caltrans posts three chain levels (R1, R2, R3); in R3 every vehicle needs chains, and 4WD/AWD vehicles must still carry chains in any chain-control area; the speed limit drops to 25–30 mph when chains are required. Check Caltrans QuickMap or call 1-800-427-7623 before driving.
T15 When to go: summer is the busiest season (Wikipedia interest peaks in July); fall brings yellow aspens in Hope Valley on Highway 88, usually best in early-to-late October [check: varies by year]; winter is ski season (Palisades, Heavenly); weekdays are quieter.
