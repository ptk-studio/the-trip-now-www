# Day 3: Truckee and the North Shore

## Truckee's Commercial Row

Truckee grew up around the Central Pacific Railroad, which arrived in 1868, the year the stop was renamed Truckee. [T10] The brick buildings of Commercial Row mostly date from the 1870s and 1880s, and the Commercial Row–Brickelltown Historic District joined the National Register in 2009. [T10]

## Donner Memorial State Park

Just west of town, Donner Memorial State Park is open year-round. [T11] Day use runs sunrise to sunset, about $10 per vehicle, and the visitor center is open daily 10 am to 5 pm, its museum and gift shop in winter too; it closes on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. [T11] The campgrounds are seasonal; the lakefront paved trail stays open. [T11]

> **Did you know?** The Donner Party, about 81–87 emigrants, was trapped here by early snow in late 1846. [T11] The first relief reached them in mid-February 1847, and about 45 survived. [T11]

## Option: Sand Harbor

From the North Shore, Sand Harbor (a Nevada state park on the East Shore) is a classic beach stop. [T9] From April 15 to October 15, you need a day-use reservation to drive in between 8 and 10:30 am; after 10:30 it's first come, first served. [T9] Entry was about $10 for Nevada-plated cars and $15 for others in 2025, so check this year's fee. [T9]

## Where to stay

- **The Truckee Hotel** (budget–mid, $–$$): founded in 1873 as a stagecoach stop; no elevator, some train noise. [STAY-TRUCKEE]
- **Gravity Haus Truckee-Tahoe** (mid–upper, $$–$$$): formerly the Cedar House Sport Hotel. [STAY-TRUCKEE]

## Where to eat

- **Squeeze In** ($): omelettes since 1974; get there before 9 to skip the line. [EAT-TRUCKEE]
- **Moody's Bistro, Bar & Beats** ($$–$$$): dinner in the Truckee Hotel, live music Thursday to Saturday. [EAT-TRUCKEE]

Prices, hours and seasons change, so check before you go.
