FACTS (DRAFT for review; checked 2026-11-15; not yet confirmed by the Owner):
N1 Getting there: downtown Napa is about 50 miles north of San Francisco; about 1 hour on a weekday, up to about 2 hours on weekends. Two routes: over the Golden Gate Bridge (US-101 → CA-37 → CA-121/CA-29), or over the Bay Bridge through the East Bay (I-80). Sonoma is about 45 miles from San Francisco, about 1–1.5 hours via US-101 → CA-37 → CA-121 → CA-12; Napa to Sonoma is about 12–15 miles, about 20–30 minutes, via CA-121/CA-12. Without a car: the San Francisco Bay Ferry from the Ferry Building to Vallejo, then VINE Transit Route 11 (Napa–Vallejo Connector) into Napa. Traffic varies; avoid weekday rush hours.
N2 Oxbow Public Market (downtown Napa): 610 & 644 First St, on the Napa River; open daily about 07:30–21:00 (vendors set their own hours; closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day); free lots on the north and south sides. Vendors include Gott's Roadside, Hog Island Oyster Bar, The Model Bakery, The Fatted Calf, Fieldwork Brewing and Ritual Coffee. DID YOU KNOW: the Oxbow District takes its name from the bend the Napa River makes here; the market's developer, Steve Carlin, had earlier managed San Francisco's Ferry Building Marketplace.
N3 Carneros (south of Napa): the Los Carneros wine region (an official AVA since 1983) is the only one that crosses both Napa and Sonoma counties; its cool, bay-influenced climate suits sparkling wine. Domaine Carneros, its best-known château, was founded in 1987 by the family behind Champagne Taittinger; seated tastings only, reservations required, daily from 10:00 (last reservation 17:00); guests 21+ only; tasting fees not published on its visit page, check before you go. DID YOU KNOW: "Los Carneros" is Spanish for "the rams", after the sheep that once grazed these hills; the château is modelled on Taittinger's Château de la Marquetterie in Champagne.
N4 Tasting fees and reservations (check before you go): in 2025 the average standard tasting fee was about $79 in Napa and about $47 in Sonoma (reserve tastings in Sonoma about $95), the highest in the US (Silicon Valley Bank's 2026 wine report, June 2026). Expect roughly $40–$100+ per person per winery; many wineries book seated tastings ahead, and walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. Sonoma's town tasting rooms are more often walk-in friendly; groups of 5 or more should always book.
N5 Highway 29 and the Silverado Trail: two parallel roads run up the valley from Napa to Calistoga (the valley is about 35 miles from end to end). Highway 29 passes Yountville, Oakville, Rutherford and St. Helena and is the busier road; the Silverado Trail on the east side is usually quieter. Cross roads link the two, so you can go up one and back on the other.
N6 Robert Mondavi Winery (Oakville, on Highway 29): built in 1966, with the arch and bell tower designed with Cliff May; reopened on April 20, 2026, after a three-year renovation, for its 60th anniversary. Entry-level Pioneer Tasting about $60 per person (about 60 min); booking ahead recommended, walk-ins when there's room. DID YOU KNOW: the estate sits in the To Kalon Vineyard, from the ancient Greek for "the highest beauty".
N7 St. Helena: a walkable Main Street halfway up the valley. Charles Krug Winery (1861) is usually called Napa Valley's oldest winery and is California Historical Landmark No. 563; the Mondavi family bought it in 1943. DID YOU KNOW: Gott's Roadside on Main Street serves burgers from the 1949 Taylor's Refresher drive-in stand; the old Taylor's sign is a town historic landmark.
N8 Napa Valley Wine Train (option for Day 2): leaves downtown Napa (1275 McKinstry St) on a 36-mile round trip to St. Helena and back, past Yountville, Oakville and Rutherford; the lunch train runs about 3 hours (about 11:30–14:30). Fares vary by experience and date, roughly $200 for a basic dinner trip to $800+ for winery packages; book ahead and check live prices. DID YOU KNOW: the line was founded in 1864 by Samuel Brannan to carry visitors to his Calistoga hot springs; the train uses restored 1915–1917 Pullman dining cars and has carried wine-country guests since September 1989.
N9 Castello di Amorosa (near Calistoga): a castle winery in 14th-century Tuscan style; open daily about 10:00–17:30, by reservation only. Entry with a wine tasting from about $40; guided castle tour with tasting from about $55 (check before you go). DID YOU KNOW: owner Dario Sattui began building in 1994 and opened it in April 2007; it has 107 rooms on eight levels, built from about 8,000 tons of hand-chiseled stone and hundreds of thousands of antique European bricks.
N10 Calistoga (top of the valley): known for hot springs and mud baths at spas along Lincoln Avenue, such as Indian Springs and Dr. Wilkinson's (mud baths since 1952); book ahead, prices vary by package. DID YOU KNOW: Sam Brannan opened a hot-springs resort here in the early 1860s; the popular story is that he meant to say "the Saratoga of California" and it came out "the Calistoga of Sarafornia". Calistoga's Chateau Montelena made the 1973 Chardonnay that beat the top French whites at the "Judgment of Paris" blind tasting on May 24, 1976.
N11 Sonoma Plaza and the Mission: the 8-acre plaza, laid out by General Mariano Vallejo in 1835, is the largest of its kind in California and a National Historic Landmark (1961). Sonoma State Historic Park (the Mission, the Sonoma Barracks and Vallejo's sites) is open daily 10:00–17:00, about $3 per adult (closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day); free parking behind the Barracks. Mission San Francisco Solano (1823) was the 21st, last and northernmost of California's missions, and the only one founded under Mexican rule. Many tasting rooms ring the plaza; several take walk-ins (book on weekends). DID YOU KNOW: on June 14, 1846, American settlers raised the Bear Flag on this plaza in the Bear Flag Revolt; the bear is still on California's state flag.
N12 Jack London State Historic Park (Glen Ellen, a short drive from the Plaza): open daily 09:00–17:00 (closed Christmas); House of Happy Walls museum 10:00–17:00; day-use fee per vehicle (check before you go). Trails lead through his Beauty Ranch to the ruins of Wolf House. DID YOU KNOW: Wolf House, the Londons' stone dream home, burned on the night of August 22, 1913, shortly before they were due to move in; Jack London's ashes lie on a nearby knoll under a rock from the house, and Charmian later built the House of Happy Walls.
N13 Healdsburg (option for Day 3): about 50 minutes north of Sonoma via CA-12 and US-101, in northern Sonoma County; a walkable town around a central plaza laid out in 1857 by Harmon Heald, ringed by tasting rooms, galleries and restaurants. DID YOU KNOW: tiny Healdsburg has two Michelin-starred restaurants in 2026: SingleThread (three stars) and Troubadour Bread & Bistro (its first star in 2026).
N14 When to go: harvest ("crush") runs from late summer through fall (sparkling-wine grapes are often picked from early August; Cabernet Sauvignon last, into October or later); it is the peak travel season, so book tastings, hotels and dinners well ahead. In 2026 the crush began early, on July 21. Mustard season, when wild yellow mustard blooms between the vines, is January through March, peaking about mid-February. Weekdays are quieter than weekends all year.
N15 Don't drink and drive: in California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or more (0.01% if you are under 21). Tastings add up, so choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver (private chauffeur services, the open-air Napa Valley Wine Trolley, or the Wine Train), or use Uber/Lyft. VINE Transit's Route 10 (Up Valley Connector) runs daily up the valley, with local shuttles in Yountville, St. Helena and Calistoga; the Napa Valley Vine Trail, a paved path, suits cycling from Napa to Yountville.
N16 Services and practical points: you must be 21 or older to taste (bring ID); the valley towns (downtown Napa, Yountville, St. Helena, Calistoga, Sonoma) are walkable, with ordinary town services; many wineries and the Oxbow market close or shorten hours on Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve/Day and New Year's Day. Book dinners ahead on weekends and in harvest season.
