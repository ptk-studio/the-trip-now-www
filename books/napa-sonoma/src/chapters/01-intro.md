# Napa & Sonoma Wine Country in 3 Days

This guide is for first-time and returning visitors who want a relaxed, well-paced long weekend in wine country. You get three days and plenty of room to linger.

## The 3-day shape

- **Day 1:** Napa and Carneros.
- **Day 2:** Up the valley.
- **Day 3:** Sonoma and Glen Ellen.

## Getting there

- **To Napa:** Downtown Napa is about 50 miles north of San Francisco. Allow about 1 hour on a weekday and up to about 2 hours on weekends. [N1]
- **Two routes:** Cross the Golden Gate Bridge (US-101 → CA-37 → CA-121/CA-29), or take the Bay Bridge through the East Bay (I-80). [N1]
- **To Sonoma:** Sonoma is about 45 miles from San Francisco, about 1–1.5 hours via US-101 → CA-37 → CA-121 → CA-12. [N1]
- **Napa to Sonoma:** Plan on about 12–15 miles, or about 20–30 minutes, via CA-121/CA-12. [N1]
- **Without a car:** Take the San Francisco Bay Ferry from the Ferry Building to Vallejo, then VINE Transit Route 11 (Napa–Vallejo Connector) into Napa. [N1]
- **Traffic:** It varies, so avoid weekday rush hours. [N1]

## When to go

Harvest ("crush") runs from late summer through fall. Cabernet Sauvignon is picked last, into October or later. [N14] It is the peak travel season, so book tastings, hotels and dinners well ahead. [N14] In 2026 the crush began early, on July 21. [N14]

Mustard season, when wild yellow mustard blooms between the vines, runs January through March and peaks about mid-February. [N14] Weekdays are quieter than weekends all year. [N14]

## Book your tastings ahead

Expect roughly $40–$100+ per person per winery. [N4] In 2025 the average standard tasting fee was about $79 in Napa and about $47 in Sonoma. [N4] Reserve tastings in Sonoma averaged about $95. [N4]

Many wineries book seated tastings ahead, and walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. [N4] Sonoma's town tasting rooms are more often walk-in friendly. [N4] Groups of 5 or more should always book. [N4]

## Don't drive after tasting

The driver doesn't taste. In California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08% or more (0.01% if you are under 21). [N15] Choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver (private chauffeur services, the open-air Napa Valley Wine Trolley, or the Wine Train), or use Uber/Lyft. [N15] Tasting is for guests 21 and older, so bring ID. [N16]

Prices, hours and reservation rules change, so check before you go.
