# Day 1: Napa and Carneros

## Oxbow Public Market

Start in downtown Napa at Oxbow Public Market, at 610 & 644 First St on the Napa River. [N2] It is open daily about 07:30-21:00, though vendors set their own hours. [N2] It closes on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. [N2] Free lots sit on the north and south sides. [N2]

> **Did you know?** The Oxbow District takes its name from the bend the Napa River makes here. [N2] The market's developer, Steve Carlin, had earlier managed San Francisco's Ferry Building Marketplace. [N2]

## A Carneros tasting

Head south of Napa to Carneros. The Los Carneros wine region has been an official AVA since 1983 and is the only one that crosses both Napa and Sonoma counties. [N3] Its cool, bay-influenced climate suits sparkling wine. [N3]

Domaine Carneros is its best-known château. [N3] It was founded in 1987 by the family behind Champagne Taittinger. [N3] Tastings are seated only and reservations are required. [N3] It is open daily from 10:00, with the last reservation at 17:00. [N3] Tasting fees aren't published on its visit page, so check before you go. [N3]

Tasting is for guests 21 and older, so bring ID. [N16] The driver doesn't taste: choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver, or use Uber/Lyft, because in California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08% or more. [N15]

> **Did you know?** "Los Carneros" is Spanish for "the rams", after the sheep that once grazed these hills. [N3]

## How tasting works

Fees vary widely. In 2025 the average standard tasting fee in Napa was about $79. [N4] Expect roughly $40-$100+ per person per winery. [N4] Many wineries book seated tastings ahead, and walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. [N4] Groups of 5 or more should always book. [N4] Fees and reservation rules change, so check before you go. [N4]
