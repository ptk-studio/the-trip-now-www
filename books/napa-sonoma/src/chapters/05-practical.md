# Practical Matters: Getting There, Getting Around, Booking

## Getting around without driving

**The driver doesn't taste.** In California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or more (0.01% if you are under 21), so choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver, or use a rideshare. [N15]

Your options:

- Choose a designated driver, because tastings add up. [N15]
- Take a tour with a driver: a private chauffeur service, the open-air Napa Valley Wine Trolley, or the Wine Train. [N15]
- Use Uber or Lyft. [N15]
- Ride VINE Transit's Route 10 (Up Valley Connector), which runs up the valley. The current timetable is at vinetransit.com, so check before you go. [N15]
- Cycle the Napa Valley Vine Trail, a paved path that suits the ride from Napa to Yountville. [N15]

The valley towns (downtown Napa, Yountville, St. Helena, Calistoga and Sonoma) are walkable and have ordinary town services. [N16]

## Age and ID

You must be 21 or older to taste, so bring ID. [N16]

## Getting there

Downtown Napa is about 50 miles north of San Francisco. The drive takes about 1 hour on a weekday and up to about 2 hours on weekends. [N1]

You have two routes to Napa:

- Over the Golden Gate Bridge: US-101, then CA-37, then CA-121/CA-29. [N1]
- Over the Bay Bridge through the East Bay on I-80. [N1]

Sonoma is about 45 miles from San Francisco, about 1 to 1.5 hours via US-101, CA-37, CA-121 and CA-12. [N1] Napa to Sonoma is about 12 to 15 miles, about 20 to 30 minutes, via CA-121/CA-12. [N1]

Without a car, take the San Francisco Bay Ferry from the Ferry Building to Vallejo. Then take VINE Transit Route 11 (the Napa–Vallejo Connector) into Napa. [N1]

Traffic varies, so avoid weekday rush hours. [N1]

## Reservations and tasting fees

Expect roughly $40 to $100+ per person per winery. [N4] In 2025 the average standard tasting fee was about $79 in Napa and about $47 in Sonoma, and reserve tastings in Sonoma averaged about $95. [N4] Fees change, so check before you go. [N4]

- Many wineries book seated tastings ahead. Walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. [N4]
- Sonoma's town tasting rooms are more often walk-in friendly. [N4]
- Groups of 5 or more should always book. [N4]

## Holidays and dinners

Many wineries and the Oxbow market close or shorten hours on Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and Day, and New Year's Day. [N16]

Book dinners ahead on weekends and in harvest season. [N16]

Prices, hours and reservation rules change, so check before you go.
