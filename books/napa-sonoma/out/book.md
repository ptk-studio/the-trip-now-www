# Napa & Sonoma Wine Country in 3 Days

This guide is for first-time and returning visitors who want a relaxed, well-paced long weekend in wine country. You get three days and plenty of room to linger.

## The 3-day shape

- **Day 1:** Napa and Carneros.
- **Day 2:** Up the valley.
- **Day 3:** Sonoma and Glen Ellen.

## Getting there

- **To Napa:** Downtown Napa is about 50 miles north of San Francisco. Allow about 1 hour on a weekday and up to about 2 hours on weekends.
- **Two routes:** Cross the Golden Gate Bridge (US-101, then CA-37, then CA-121/CA-29), or take the Bay Bridge through the East Bay (I-80).
- **To Sonoma:** Sonoma is about 45 miles from San Francisco, about 1–1.5 hours via US-101, then CA-37, CA-121 and CA-12.
- **Napa to Sonoma:** Plan on about 12–15 miles, or about 20–30 minutes, via CA-121/CA-12.
- **Without a car:** Take the San Francisco Bay Ferry from the Ferry Building to Vallejo, then VINE Transit Route 11 (Napa–Vallejo Connector) into Napa.
- **Traffic:** It varies, so avoid weekday rush hours.

## When to go

Harvest ("crush") runs from late summer through fall. Cabernet Sauvignon is picked last, into October or later. It is the peak travel season, so book tastings, hotels and dinners well ahead. In 2026 the crush began early, on July 21.

Mustard season, when wild yellow mustard blooms between the vines, runs January through March and peaks about mid-February. Weekdays are quieter than weekends all year.

## Book your tastings ahead

Expect roughly $40–$100+ per person per winery. In 2025 the average standard tasting fee was about $79 in Napa and about $47 in Sonoma. Reserve tastings in Sonoma averaged about $95.

Many wineries book seated tastings ahead, and walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. Sonoma's town tasting rooms are more often walk-in friendly. Groups of 5 or more should always book.

## Don't drive after tasting

The driver doesn't taste. In California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08% or more (0.01% if you are under 21). Choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver (private chauffeur services, the open-air Napa Valley Wine Trolley, or the Wine Train), or use Uber/Lyft. Tasting is for guests 21 and older, so bring ID.

Prices, hours and reservation rules change, so check before you go.

# Day 1: Napa and Carneros

## Oxbow Public Market

Start in downtown Napa at Oxbow Public Market, at 610 & 644 First St on the Napa River. It is open daily about 07:30-21:00, though vendors set their own hours. It closes on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. Free lots sit on the north and south sides.

> **Did you know?** The Oxbow District takes its name from the bend the Napa River makes here. The market's developer, Steve Carlin, had earlier managed San Francisco's Ferry Building Marketplace.

## A Carneros tasting

Head south of Napa to Carneros. The Los Carneros wine region has been an official AVA since 1983 and is the only one that crosses both Napa and Sonoma counties. Its cool, bay-influenced climate suits sparkling wine.

Domaine Carneros is its best-known château. It was founded in 1987 by the family behind Champagne Taittinger. Tastings are seated only and reservations are required. It is open daily from 10:00, with the last reservation at 17:00. Tasting fees aren't published on its visit page, so check before you go.

Tasting is for guests 21 and older, so bring ID. The driver doesn't taste: choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver, or use Uber/Lyft, because in California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08% or more.

> **Did you know?** "Los Carneros" is Spanish for "the rams", after the sheep that once grazed these hills.

## How tasting works

Fees vary widely. In 2025 the average standard tasting fee in Napa was about $79. Expect roughly $40-$100+ per person per winery. Many wineries book seated tastings ahead, and walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. Groups of 5 or more should always book. Fees and reservation rules change, so check before you go.

# Day 2: Up the Valley

![](/Users/thomas/workspaces/web-worker2/the-trip-now-www/books/napa-sonoma/out/epub-img/day2-valley.jpg)

Today you head north from Napa toward Calistoga, with a historic winery, a castle and a spa town along the way.

## The two roads up the valley

- Two parallel roads run from Napa to Calistoga, and the valley is about 35 miles from end to end.
- Highway 29 passes Yountville, Oakville, Rutherford and St. Helena, and it is the busier road.
- The Silverado Trail on the east side is usually quieter.
- Cross roads link the two, so you can go up one and come back on the other.

The driver doesn't taste. Choose a designated driver, book a tour with a driver, or use Uber/Lyft, because driving with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08% or more is illegal in California. Tasting is for guests 21 and older, so bring ID.

## Robert Mondavi Winery, Oakville

- The winery stands in Oakville on Highway 29. It was built in 1966, with an arch and bell tower designed with Cliff May.
- It reopened on April 20, 2026, after a three-year renovation, for its 60th anniversary.
- The entry-level Pioneer Tasting costs about $60 per person and takes about 60 minutes.
- Book ahead if you can. Walk-ins are welcome when there's room.

> **Did you know?** The estate sits in the To Kalon Vineyard, a name from the ancient Greek for "the highest beauty".

## St. Helena

- St. Helena has a walkable Main Street halfway up the valley.
- Charles Krug Winery was founded in the mid-1800s and is one of Napa's oldest wineries. It is California Historical Landmark No. 563.
- The Mondavi family bought it in 1943.

> **Did you know?** A burger stand on Main Street serves burgers from the 1949 Taylor's Refresher drive-in stand, and the old Taylor's sign is a town historic landmark.

## Option: the Napa Valley Wine Train

If you would rather not drive up the valley, the train is one way to do it.

- It leaves downtown Napa (1275 McKinstry St) on a 36-mile round trip to St. Helena and back, past Yountville, Oakville and Rutherford.
- The lunch train runs about 3 hours, from about 11:30 to 14:30.
- Fares run from about $200 to $800+ depending on the experience and date. Book ahead.

> **Did you know?** The line was founded in 1864 by Samuel Brannan to carry visitors to his Calistoga hot springs. The train uses restored 1915–1917 Pullman dining cars and has carried wine-country guests since September 1989.

## Castello di Amorosa, near Calistoga

- This castle winery is built in 14th-century Tuscan style.
- It is open daily from about 10:00 to 17:30, by reservation only.
- Tastings start at about $40. Guided castle tours with a tasting start at about $55.

> **Did you know?** Owner Dario Sattui began building in 1994 and opened the castle in April 2007. It has 107 rooms on eight levels.

## Calistoga

- Calistoga sits at the top of the valley.
- It is known for hot springs and mud baths at spas along Lincoln Avenue.
- Book a spa ahead. Prices vary by package.

> **Did you know?** Sam Brannan opened a hot-springs resort here in the early 1860s. The popular story is that he meant to say "the Saratoga of California" and it came out "the Calistoga of Sarafornia". Calistoga's Chateau Montelena made the 1973 Chardonnay that beat the top French whites at the "Judgment of Paris" blind tasting on May 24, 1976.

## Where to stay

- **Bothe-Napa Valley State Park** (budget, $): campsites, restored historic cabins and yurts in a state park between St. Helena and Calistoga. Book on ReserveCalifornia.
- **Dr. Wilkinson's Backyard Resort & Mineral Springs, Calistoga** (mid, $$): walk to town, with mud baths and mineral pools.
- **Calistoga Motor Lodge & Spa** (mid, $$): 50 rooms, two hot-spring mineral pools and a spa.
- **Indian Springs Calistoga** (upper, $$$): California's oldest continuously operating thermal pool and spa.

## Where to eat

- **Gott's Roadside, St. Helena** ($): the original 1949 roadside stand.
- **Farmstead at Long Meadow Ranch, St. Helena** ($$–$$$): the ranch's own restaurant on Main Street.
- **Cook St. Helena** ($$): a local favourite with a long marble bar facing the open kitchen.

Prices, hours and reservation rules change, so check before you go.

# Day 3: Sonoma and Glen Ellen

![](/Users/thomas/workspaces/web-worker2/the-trip-now-www/books/napa-sonoma/out/epub-img/day3-sonoma.jpg)

## Sonoma Plaza and the Mission

Start on Sonoma Plaza. General Mariano Vallejo laid out the 8-acre square in 1835. It is the largest of its kind in California and a National Historic Landmark (1961).

Sonoma State Historic Park covers the Mission, the Sonoma Barracks and Vallejo's sites. It is open daily 10:00-17:00 and costs about $3 per adult. It closes on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. Parking is free behind the Barracks.

Mission San Francisco Solano (1823) was the 21st, last and northernmost of California's missions. It was also the only one founded under Mexican rule.

Many tasting rooms ring the plaza. Several take walk-ins, but book on weekends.

The driver doesn't taste. In California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08% or more, so choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver, or use Uber/Lyft. Tasting is for guests 21 and older, so bring ID.

> **Did you know?** On June 14, 1846, American settlers raised the Bear Flag on this plaza in the Bear Flag Revolt. The bear is still on California's state flag.

## Jack London State Historic Park, Glen Ellen

Drive a short way from the Plaza to Glen Ellen. The park is open daily 09:00-17:00 and closed on Christmas. The House of Happy Walls museum is open 10:00-17:00. The day-use fee is about $10-15 per vehicle, so check before you go.

Trails lead through Jack London's Beauty Ranch to the ruins of Wolf House. Wear comfortable shoes and bring water.

> **Did you know?** Wolf House, the Londons' stone dream home, burned on the night of August 22, 1913, shortly before they were due to move in. Jack London's ashes lie on a nearby knoll under a rock from the house, and Charmian later built the House of Happy Walls.

## Option: Healdsburg

If you want a longer day, Healdsburg is about 50 minutes north of Sonoma via CA-12 and US-101, in northern Sonoma County. It is a walkable town around a central plaza laid out in 1857 by Harmon Heald. Tasting rooms, galleries and restaurants ring the plaza. Plan on a full outing, since the drive adds about 50 minutes each way.

> **Did you know?** Tiny Healdsburg has two Michelin-starred restaurants in 2026. One holds three stars, and the other earned its first star in 2026.

## Where to stay

- Sonoma Creek Inn, Boyes Hot Springs: budget; a clean retro roadside motel a few miles north of the Plaza; $.
- El Dorado Hotel: mid; on Sonoma Plaza; $$.
- Fairmont Sonoma Mission Inn & Spa: upper; a thermal-spring spa resort about 2 miles from the Plaza; $$$.
- MacArthur Place Hotel & Spa: splurge; a spa hotel about 0.6 mile from the Plaza, with a nightly amenity fee; $$$.

## Where to eat

- El Molino Central, Boyes Hot Springs: Michelin Bib Gourmand 2026; handmade tortillas, tamales and tacos; order at the counter; $.
- The Girl & the Fig: on Sonoma Plaza; French-inspired California cooking, charcuterie, Rhône wines and outdoor seating; $$.
- Glen Ellen Star, Glen Ellen: Michelin Bib Gourmand 2026; wood-fired vegetables and pizzas in a small dining room, near Jack London park; $$.

Prices, hours and reservation rules change, so check before you go.

# Practical Matters: Getting There, Getting Around, Booking

## Getting around without driving

**The driver doesn't taste.** In California it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or more (0.01% if you are under 21), so choose a designated driver, take a tour with a driver, or use a rideshare.

Your options:

- Choose a designated driver, because tastings add up.
- Take a tour with a driver: a private chauffeur service, the open-air Napa Valley Wine Trolley, or the Wine Train.
- Use Uber or Lyft.
- Ride VINE Transit's Route 10 (Up Valley Connector), which runs up the valley. The current timetable is at vinetransit.com, so check before you go.
- Cycle the Napa Valley Vine Trail, a paved path that suits the ride from Napa to Yountville.

The valley towns (downtown Napa, Yountville, St. Helena, Calistoga and Sonoma) are walkable and have ordinary town services.

## Age and ID

You must be 21 or older to taste, so bring ID.

## Getting there

Downtown Napa is about 50 miles north of San Francisco. The drive takes about 1 hour on a weekday and up to about 2 hours on weekends.

You have two routes to Napa:

- Over the Golden Gate Bridge: US-101, then CA-37, then CA-121/CA-29.
- Over the Bay Bridge through the East Bay on I-80.

Sonoma is about 45 miles from San Francisco, about 1 to 1.5 hours via US-101, CA-37, CA-121 and CA-12. Napa to Sonoma is about 12 to 15 miles, about 20 to 30 minutes, via CA-121/CA-12.

Without a car, take the San Francisco Bay Ferry from the Ferry Building to Vallejo. Then take VINE Transit Route 11 (the Napa–Vallejo Connector) into Napa.

Traffic varies, so avoid weekday rush hours.

# Packing for Wine Country

Pack light and plan for a day that runs from morning to evening. This list covers what you'll want in the car or bag.

## Everyday items

- Comfortable shoes for walking town plazas, vineyards and trails
- Layers, such as a light jacket or sweater, for changeable temperatures
- A refillable water bottle, and water in the car
- Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat
- Phone, charger and a car charger or power bank
- A small day bag or tote for your essentials
- A cooler bag, if you plan to carry purchases between stops

## ID for tastings

Bring a photo ID. You must be 21 or older to taste, and you may be asked to show ID.

Keep your confirmations easy to find, on your phone or printed. Many wineries book seated tastings ahead, and walk-in space is limited, especially on weekends and in harvest season. Groups of 5 or more should always book. Expect roughly $40–$100+ per person per winery.
