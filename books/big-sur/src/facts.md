FACTS (the only facts you may use; checked 2026-11-14/15; the Owner confirmed every item):
B1 Getting there: San Francisco to Monterey is about 113–120 miles; about 2–2.5 hours via US-101 → CA-156 → CA-1, or about 2.5–3 hours all on Highway 1 (traffic varies).
B2 Cannery Row (Monterey): sardine canning from 1902; the canneries had closed by the mid-1960s. DID YOU KNOW: the street was called Ocean View Avenue until 1958, when it was renamed after John Steinbeck's 1945 novel "Cannery Row".
B3 Monterey Bay Aquarium (on Cannery Row): opened 1984 on the old Hovden sardine cannery site; adult ticket about $65; usually open about 10:00–17:00; book ahead. DID YOU KNOW: the cannery's boiler house and smokestacks were kept; the Kelp Forest tank is about 28 ft tall, one of the first to keep a living kelp forest.
B4 17-Mile Drive (Pebble Beach): a private toll road; about $12–12.50 per car at the gate; open sunrise to sunset. DID YOU KNOW: the Lone Cypress on its rock above the surf has been the Pebble Beach Company's logo since about 1919; photos for yourself are fine, commercial use of its image isn't.
B5 Carmel-by-the-Sea: houses have names instead of street numbers; Hugh Comstock's fairy-tale cottages (1924–28), e.g. Hansel and Gretel on Torres Street between 5th and 6th; Carmel Beach welcomes dogs. DID YOU KNOW: Clint Eastwood was the town's mayor from 1986 to 1988. Comstock built the first storybook cottage for his wife's handmade dolls.
B6 Point Lobos State Natural Reserve (just south of Carmel): day use only; open about 08:00 to sunset (about 19:00 in summer); about $10 per car; the lot fills early, so arrive early or park on Highway 1 and walk in; no vehicles over 21 ft inside. DID YOU KNOW: the little Whalers Cabin was built in the 1850s by Chinese fishermen; Portuguese shore whalers used the cove from 1862; today it is a small museum.
B7 Bixby Creek Bridge: completed 1932; about 714 ft long and about 260 ft above the creek; small pull-outs with limited parking; never stop on the bridge. DID YOU KNOW: it opened on Nov 27, 1932, for about $200,000, so the new coast road (Highway 1, finished in 1937) could cross Bixby Creek.
B8 McWay Falls (Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park): a short overlook walk, about 0.6 mile round trip; day use about $10 per car, and the same day's receipt also works at Point Lobos, Pfeiffer Big Sur and Andrew Molera. DID YOU KNOW: above the falls stood the 1940 "Waterfall House" of Lathrop and Helen Hooper Brown; Helen gave the land to the state, asking that the park be named for her neighbour, the pioneer rancher Julia Pfeiffer Burns; the house was torn down in the mid-1960s.
B9 Pfeiffer Beach (Los Padres National Forest): reached by narrow Sycamore Canyon Road (about 2.2 miles, no RVs or trailers); day use about $15; a 65-car lot that is full on summer weekends. DID YOU KNOW: the purple streaks in the sand come from manganese garnet washed down from the hills; the Keyhole Arch glows at sunset around the winter solstice.
B10 Highway 1 closures: slides and fires close stretches of Highway 1 (in 2026: Regent's Slide early in the year, fire-support closures in Aug–Sep). Always check Caltrans QuickMap (quickmap.dot.ca.gov) before you go.
B11 Services in Big Sur: very limited cell service; few, pricey gas stations, so fill up in Carmel or Monterey; download offline maps.
B12 When to go: September–October is clearest with fewer crowds; summer is busiest with frequent morning fog; November–March is the rainy season, when slides are likelier.
B13 Nepenthe (Big Sur restaurant, since 1949): known for the Ambrosia Burger on the cliff terrace; $$–$$$. DID YOU KNOW: it opened on April 24, 1949, and its terrace appears in the 1965 film "The Sandpiper".
STAY-MONTEREY: HI Monterey Hostel, 778 Hawthorne St, a block from Cannery Row (budget; private rooms or dorms); Asilomar Hotel & Conference Grounds, Pacific Grove (mid; historic lodge buildings in the dunes on a state-park beach); Monterey Plaza Hotel & Spa, 400 Cannery Row (splurge; built over the water).
STAY-CARMEL: Cypress Inn (mid–upper; in the village; Doris Day's hotel; pet-friendly); Mission Ranch (mid; Clint Eastwood's restored ranch with sheep in the meadow and views of Point Lobos).
STAY-BIGSUR: Big Sur Lodge (mid; the only lodging inside Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park; books far ahead); Glen Oaks Big Sur (mid–upper; 1950s adobe motor lodge and cabins in the redwoods); Deetjen's Big Sur Inn (budget–mid for Big Sur; 1930s inn, no Wi-Fi or cell); Post Ranch Inn (splurge; cliff-top rooms over the Pacific).
EAT-MONTEREY: Old Fisherman's Grotto on the wharf (since 1950; clam chowder in a sourdough bowl; $$).
EAT-CARMEL: Hog's Breath Inn (founded by Clint Eastwood in 1970; courtyard with fire pits; steak or chowder; $$–$$$); Mission Ranch restaurant (sunset dinner with the meadow and Point Lobos view; piano bar; $$$).
EAT-BIGSUR: Nepenthe (see B13); Big Sur Bakery (morning pastries, wood-fired lunch; $–$$; closed Tuesdays); Deetjen's dining room (breakfast; eggs Benedict is the classic; $$).
