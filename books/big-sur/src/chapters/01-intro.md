# Introduction and Before You Go

## Who this book is for

This pocket itinerary is for travelers who want the best of the Monterey coast and Big Sur in three unhurried days. You'll need a car, comfortable shoes and a taste for scenic driving. You don't need to be an expert hiker.

## The plan at a glance

- **Day 1, Monterey:** Cannery Row, the aquarium and 17-Mile Drive.
- **Day 2, Carmel and Point Lobos:** Carmel-by-the-Sea in the morning, then Point Lobos.
- **Day 3, Big Sur:** Bixby Bridge, Pfeiffer Beach, lunch in Big Sur and McWay Falls.

Each day is short enough to leave room for slow mornings, long views and a second look at anything you love.

## Getting there

San Francisco to Monterey is about 113–120 miles. [B1] Allow about 2–2.5 hours via US-101, CA-156 and CA-1, or about 2.5–3 hours if you stay on Highway 1 the whole way. Traffic varies. [B1]

## When to go

September–October is clearest, with fewer crowds. [B12] Summer is the busiest season, with frequent morning fog. [B12] November–March is the rainy season, when slides are likelier. [B12] Bring layers whatever the month, because the coast can turn cool and breezy quickly.

## Highway 1 road status

Slides and fires close stretches of Highway 1. [B10] In 2026, Regent's Slide closed a section early in the year, and fire-support closures followed in August and September. [B10] Always check Caltrans QuickMap (quickmap.dot.ca.gov) before you go, and check it again the morning you drive south. [B10]

## Services in Big Sur

Cell service in Big Sur is very limited. [B11] Gas stations are few and pricey, so fill up in Carmel or Monterey. [B11] Download offline maps before you leave. [B11] Carry water and a few snacks too, so a slow stretch of road doesn't spoil your day.

## Before you set off

- Check QuickMap for closures.
- Fill the tank in Monterey or Carmel.
- Download offline maps.
- Pack layers, water and comfortable shoes.

Prices, hours and road status change, so check before you go.
