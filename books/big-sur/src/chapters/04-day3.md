# Day 3: Big Sur

Today you drive south on Highway 1 along the Big Sur coast. Plan for slow miles and frequent stops.

## Before you set out

Slides and fires close stretches of Highway 1. In 2026 these included Regent's Slide early in the year and fire-support closures in August and September. Check Caltrans QuickMap (quickmap.dot.ca.gov) before you go. [B10]

Cell service in Big Sur is very limited, so download offline maps. Gas stations are few and pricey, so fill up in Carmel or Monterey. [B11] Bring water, layers and comfortable shoes.

## Bixby Creek Bridge

Your first big photo stop is Bixby Creek Bridge, completed in 1932. It is about 714 ft long and about 260 ft above the creek. [B7] Use the small pull-outs, where parking is limited, and never stop on the bridge itself. [B7]

> **Did you know?** The bridge opened on Nov 27, 1932, for about $200,000, so the new coast road (Highway 1, finished in 1937) could cross Bixby Creek. [B7]

## Pfeiffer Beach

Pfeiffer Beach sits in Los Padres National Forest and is reached by narrow Sycamore Canyon Road, about 2.2 miles long. No RVs or trailers are allowed. [B9] Day use is about $15. [B9] The lot holds 65 cars and is full on summer weekends, so come early or choose another day. [B9]

> **Did you know?** The purple streaks in the sand come from manganese garnet washed down from the hills. The Keyhole Arch glows at sunset around the winter solstice. [B9]

## Lunch at Nepenthe

Stop for lunch at Nepenthe, serving Big Sur since 1949. It is known for the Ambrosia Burger, eaten on the cliff terrace, and prices run $$–$$$. [B13]

> **Did you know?** Nepenthe opened on April 24, 1949, and its terrace appears in the 1965 film "The Sandpiper". [B13]

## McWay Falls

Finish at McWay Falls in Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park. The overlook walk is short, about 0.6 mile round trip. [B8] Day use is about $10 per car, and the same day's receipt also works at Point Lobos, Pfeiffer Big Sur and Andrew Molera. [B8]

> **Did you know?** Above the falls stood the 1940 "Waterfall House" of Lathrop and Helen Hooper Brown. Helen gave the land to the state, asking that the park be named for her neighbour, the pioneer rancher Julia Pfeiffer Burns. The house was torn down in the mid-1960s. [B8]

## Where to stay

For anyone spending the night in Big Sur. Call ahead: fires and slides close places at short notice.

- **Big Sur Lodge** (mid): the only lodging inside Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park; books far ahead. [STAY-BIGSUR]
- **Glen Oaks Big Sur** (mid–upper): 1950s adobe motor lodge and cabins in the redwoods. [STAY-BIGSUR]
- **Deetjen's Big Sur Inn** (budget–mid for Big Sur): 1930s inn, no Wi-Fi or cell. [STAY-BIGSUR]
- **Post Ranch Inn** (splurge): cliff-top rooms over the Pacific. [STAY-BIGSUR]

## Where to eat

- **Nepenthe**: the cliff-terrace lunch above, since 1949, known for the Ambrosia Burger; $$–$$$. [EAT-BIGSUR]
- **Big Sur Bakery**: morning pastries and a wood-fired lunch; $–$$; closed Tuesdays. [EAT-BIGSUR]
- **Deetjen's dining room**: breakfast, where eggs Benedict is the classic; $$. [EAT-BIGSUR]

Prices, hours and road status change, so check before you go.
