FACTS (the only facts you may use; checked 2026-11-14):
F1 Temple of Literature (Văn Miếu): open daily about 8:00–17:00, last entry about 16:30; adult ticket about 70,000 VND; cash only; no tank tops or miniskirts.
F2 Ngọc Sơn Temple on Hoàn Kiếm Lake: ticket about 30,000–50,000 VND; viewing the red Thê Húc Bridge from the shore is free.
F3 Hồ Chí Minh Mausoleum: mornings only, free, closed Mondays and Fridays; closes for about two months each year for maintenance (usually in autumn) – check the schedule.
F4 Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre: shows last about 50 minutes, several each afternoon and evening; standard seats from about 100,000 VND; evenings often sell out from November to March – book a day ahead.
F5 Nội Bài airport to the Old Quarter: city bus 86, about 45,000 VND, about 45–60 minutes, runs to Hanoi Railway Station via the Old Quarter.
F6 Hanoi to Hạ Long by expressway: about 2.5–3 hours door to door by car or shuttle.
F7 Hạ Long Bay day cruises normally include the bay's sightseeing fee in the ticket price – ask before you pay.
F8 Visa: Vietnam issues e-visas of up to 90 days (about US$25 single entry); apply only on the official government portal and check the rules for your nationality.
F9 When to go: October to April is the most comfortable; July to November brings typhoon risk, and cruises do not sail on storm days – keep a spare day.
F10 Old Quarter streets: many street names begin with "Hàng" (goods), after the trade once sold there – e.g. Hàng Bạc (silver), Hàng Gai (silk and hemp), Hàng Mã (paper goods). "36 streets" is a traditional name; there are many more.
F11 Dishes: phở (noodle soup); bún chả (grilled pork with rice noodles and dipping broth); chả cá (turmeric fish fried with dill); egg coffee (cà phê trứng), created in Hanoi in 1946.
F12 Hạ Long Bay: UNESCO World Heritage since 1994; more than a thousand limestone islands and islets.
F13 Where to stay: the Old Quarter (walkable, lively, noisy at night); around Hoàn Kiếm Lake (central, a little calmer); the French Quarter south of the lake (broader, quieter streets).
