# Introduction and Before You Go

## Who this book is for

This pocket guide is for first-time visitors who have three days and want a clear, walkable plan: a little history, a little lake, and one unforgettable day on the water. You don't need to be a seasoned traveller. Comfortable shoes, a water bottle and sunscreen will do most of the work.

## The 3-day plan at a glance

**Day 1: Old Quarter and Hoàn Kiếm Lake.** Spend your first day on foot, wandering the Old Quarter's busy streets and strolling around the lake.

**Day 2: Temple of Literature, Ba Đình and a water puppet show.** Pair a morning of temples and monuments with an evening of traditional puppetry.

**Day 3: Hạ Long Bay day cruise.** Leave the city behind for a day among the bay's limestone scenery. Hạ Long Bay has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1994 and holds more than a thousand limestone islands and islets. [F12]

## When to go

October to April is the most comfortable time to visit. [F9] From July to November there is a risk of typhoons, and cruises do not sail on storm days. [F9] If your trip falls in that window, keep a spare day in your schedule so a cancelled cruise doesn't end your Hạ Long plans. [F9] Day 3 is the day to protect, so consider building in flexibility around it.

## Visa basics

Vietnam issues e-visas of up to 90 days, at about US$25 for a single entry (check before you go). [F8] Apply only on the official government portal. [F8] Rules differ by nationality, so confirm yours before booking flights. [F8]

## Before you leave home

- Check your passport's validity and sort out your visa first.
- Look at the weather outlook for your dates, especially if you travel in typhoon season.
- Pack light, breathable clothing, and bring a layer that covers your shoulders and knees for temple visits.
- Carry some cash in small notes. Not everywhere takes cards.

With the paperwork done and a flexible mind, you're ready for Day 1.
